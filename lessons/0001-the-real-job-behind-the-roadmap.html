Forward Deployed Engineering · Lesson 1 · about 60 minutes

The real job behind the roadmap

A 12-stage FDE roadmap by Suraj Sharma went viral on X in November 2026. Before you study any of it, check it against what FDE job ads actually ask for, and against what you've already shipped.

Words with a dotted underline have a plain-English definition. Hover, tap or tab to them. All of them are in the jargon decoder.

Your win today

By the end you'll have three things: the 12 stages sorted into core and specialist, a map of your own work against them, and one project rewritten as a deployment story you can tell in an interview.

1 · One customer, many capabilities

Palantir invented the title, so start with their definition. A regular software engineer, a "Dev", builds one capability that many customers use. A Forward Deployed Software Engineer, a "Delta", does the opposite: they enable many capabilities for a single customer."While a traditional software engineer, or 'Dev,' focuses on creating a single capability that can be used for many customers, FDSEs focus on enabling many capabilities for a single customer." Palantir Blog, 2020. The FDE embeds with that customer, sometimes in their office, sometimes in their Slack or on their infrastructure, and does whatever it takes: code, data pipelines, configuration. Then they carry what they learned back to the product team.PostHog, "WTF is a forward deployed engineer?" Travel is often 20–50% of the time.

At Palantir a Delta is paired with an "Echo", a deployment strategist who owns the relationship and whether people actually adopt the tool.Palantir Blog, "Dev versus Delta". But 58% of FDE roles are at companies of 11–200 people, where one FDE often covers both jobs. That's why ads ask for "business acumen" next to "production code".

The title now covers three different jobs. In an analysis of 1,000 FDE job ads, 60% were Builders (70–90% coding, embedded with customers), 30% were sales engineers under a new name (30–40% coding, often with a quota), and 10% built internal sales tools.Bloomberry, "What I learned analyzing 1K forward deployed engineer jobs", updated Jan 2026. This course aims at the Builder. Part of the skill is spotting the other two in an ad before you apply.

The role is also moving. In May 2026 OpenAI launched a separate Deployment Company with $4B from private equity and bought Tomoro, a firm with 150 FDEs. Anthropic announced its own services company the same month. Gergely Orosz reads this as the role drifting towards "an AI-focused solutions architect or consultant", and translates a Google Cloud FDE ad as roughly 25% coding, 50% integration and plumbing, 25% meetings.The Pragmatic Engineer, "Forward deployed engineering heats up again", May 2026. His verdict: a great fit if you like shipping end-to-end with no spec, a poor fit if you want long greenfield projects.

Hold both truths. Demand is real: FDE job ads grew 1,165% year over year in 2025, and the median advertised base salary was $173,816.Both figures from the Bloomberry analysis, Jan–Oct 2025 vs. the same period in 2024. "You will never be unemployed again" isn't. No roadmap can promise that.

3 · Sort the roadmap

For each stage of the roadmap, decide: does every FDE deployment need it, or only customers who are regulated or run their own hardware? Commit to an answer before you read the feedback. Guessing wrong and then reading why is the fastest way to learn it.

The pattern: 9 of the 12 stages are core. The three specialist ones (2, 6 and 8) are what make the roadmap look frightening, and they're the ones to leave for later. Meanwhile the most-asked-for skill, working with customers, sits at Stage 11.

4 · Map your own evidence

Now turn the roadmap on yourself. For each stage, rate the proof you could show a hiring manager today. Be strict: it's your private map, and an honest one tells you where to start.

strongSomeone could check it now: a public repo, a write-up with numbers, a shipped feature you can demo and explain.
partialYou've done the idea at small scale, for example login and sessions in your own app but never SSO for a company, or a search box but no permissions on it.
thinLittle or nothing yet. Fine: that's what the lessons are for.

How to read your map: most applicants can't show an eval or a discovery story at all, so a strong rating on Stage 10 or 11 is worth a lot. Close your core gaps before any specialist stage. The lessons follow the same order as the grid above, so the first stage you rated thin is a good place to start.

5 · Check yourself

Answer from memory. Don't scroll up first: a wrong answer you then correct sticks better than a right one you looked up.

6 · Build: one deployment story

An FDE interview doesn't ask "what's your stack?". It asks "tell me about a time you shipped into a messy environment". A deployment story answers that in four parts: who the customer was and what hurt, a constraint you didn't choose, what you shipped, and how you measured it.

A worked example from a real client project. Notice it has no number yet: that's the usual weak spot, and the honest fix is to say what you would measure.

Customer: A client's AI social-media platform. Their users were being signed out at random, mid-task.
Constraint: Another team owned the backend, and it rotated refresh tokens. I couldn't change it.
Shipped: I split the route guard into pure functions with a test for every branch, made cookie writes follow whichever response went back, and made parallel requests share one refresh.
Measured: Missing. Should be "sign-out complaints per week, before and after" or "401s per 1,000 sessions".

Now write your own, from the project you rated strongest in section 4: paid work, open source, or a side project with real users. It saves in your browser as you type.
