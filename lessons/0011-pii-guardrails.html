Forward Deployed Engineering · Lesson 11 · Stage 6 (specialist) · about 60 minutes

PII guardrails

Petra, Acme's data protection officer, has read the agent's data flow and has one question before the next contract year: why does the model provider get Jan's bank account number? It doesn't need it to say his order arrived. This lesson builds the proxy that keeps names, addresses, phone numbers, bank accounts and card numbers inside Acme, and gives them back only to the person or tool that needs them.

Words with a dotted underline have a plain-English definition. Hover, tap or tab to them. All of them are in the jargon decoder.

Your win today

A masking proxy, in Postgres and TypeScript, between Acme's agent and the model: three detectors that cover each other's gaps, checksums that tell a card number from an invoice number, placeholders that stay the same for the same person, unmasking only for Kim's review queue or a tool call and only within the same conversation, a log of every unmask that never holds the values, and a one-way copy of each email for the golden set. Then the one-page answer Petra asked for.

1 · Where personal data goes

GDPR, the EU's data protection law, starts with a wide definition: "'personal data' means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person".Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), Article 4(1). The same article lists "a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier" as examples. Acme sells to bike shops, so it's tempting to think the emails are company data. They aren't. sanne.visser@fietsplaats.example is a work address, and it still names Sanne. Here is what one December morning brings into the support inbox:

EmailPersonal data in itWhat the agent needs
c-201, Sanne at FietsplaatsHer name, work email, mobile number, and "pieter from our workshop"Order A-1001, "Saturday"
c-202, Jan at Leidse FietsenHis name and his IBAN, twice, once with spaces and once withoutOrder A-1004, Florence 3, EUR 1,299.00
c-203, Markus at Velo KölnHis name, email and phone, a colleague's name and number, and a card numberOrder A-1003, an invoice number

The right column is short. GDPR's principle of data minimisation says personal data must be "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed".GDPR, Article 5(1)(c). The model needs the order number to call get_order. It doesn't need Jan's IBAN to tell him his frames were delivered.

And the email doesn't stop at the model. Every copy is one more place to protect:

  • The model provider. What it keeps depends on your contract. Anthropic, for example, offers zero data retention (ZDR): "Under a ZDR arrangement, Anthropic does not store customer prompts or responses at rest after the API response is returned." You have to ask for it.Anthropic, "API and data retention", checked November 2026: "To request ZDR for your organization, contact the Anthropic sales team", and it "is enabled per organization". Some features and models are not covered; the page lists them.
  • Your traces. Lesson 9 left prompts out of the spans, because the conventions call them sensitive.
  • Your logs. OWASP's list of things that "should usually not be recorded directly in the logs" includes "Bank account or payment card holder data" and "Sensitive personal data and some forms of personally identifiable information (PII)".OWASP, Logging Cheat Sheet, "Data to exclude".
  • The golden set. Lesson 8 built it from real emails "with names removed". Today you learn how.

There is a security reason too. Simon Willison calls it the lethal trifecta: an agent with "Access to your private data", "Exposure to untrusted content" and "The ability to externally communicate". "If your agent combines these three features, an attacker can easily trick it into accessing your private data and sending it to that attacker."Simon Willison, "The lethal trifecta for AI agents: private data, untrusted content, and external communication" (June 2025). Acme's agent reads email from anyone, and it writes replies. Masking takes the first leg away from the model: an IBAN it never saw is an IBAN a prompt injection can't make it repeat.

2 · Five ways to hide a value

People use these words as if they meant the same thing. They don't, and the difference decides what you may do with the result.

MethodExampleCan you get it back?Use it for

Encrypt, keeping the formatNL28 XQZB 8812 0937 51Yes, with the keyOld systems that check the format
AnonymiseNo link to anyone, by anyoneNoStatistics, research, sharing

Pseudonymisation is still personal data. GDPR defines it as processing "in such a manner that the personal data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately".GDPR, Article 4(5). The vault is that additional information. And the law is clear about the result: data that "could be attributed to a natural person by the use of additional information should be considered to be information on an identifiable natural person". Only anonymous information falls outside it.GDPR, Recital 26: "The principles of data protection should therefore not apply to anonymous information". So masking lowers the risk. It doesn't switch the law off.

What about the model provider, who never sees the vault? The EDPB said in January 2025 that pseudonymised data stays personal "even if pseudonymised data and additional information are not in the hands of the same person".EDPB, Guidelines 01/2025 on Pseudonymisation, version 1 for public consultation, "Executive summary". In September 2025 the EU's Court of Justice was more careful: pseudonymised data "must not be regarded as constituting, in all cases and for every person, personal data". But it also said identifiability is assessed "from the point of view of the controller".Court of Justice of the EU, Case C-413/23 P, EDPS v SRB, 4 September 2025, press release No 107/25. The case is about the regulation for EU institutions, which uses the same definitions as GDPR. Acme is the controller, and you work for Acme. For both of you, the masked emails are personal data. Whether they are for the provider is a question for lawyers, and not a promise you should make.

A hash is not a hiding place. The US Federal Trade Commission: "hashes aren't “anonymous” and can still be used to identify users". Email addresses and phone numbers come from small, guessable sets, so anyone can hash every guess and compare.FTC, "No, hashing still doesn't make your data anonymous" (July 2024). HIPAA's guidance agrees: "a code derived from a secure hash function without a secret key (e.g., “salt”) would be considered an identifying element".US Department of Health and Human Services, "Guidance Regarding Methods for De-identification of Protected Health Information", section 3.5. The safe choice for a placeholder is a plain counter, [IBAN_1]. HIPAA's rule for re-identification codes describes it well: the code "is not derived from or related to information about the individual".45 CFR 164.514(c), quoted in the same HHS guidance, "The De-identification Standard".

Format-preserving encryption is for old systems. If a core banking system rejects anything that isn't IBAN-shaped, FPE gives it an IBAN-shaped ciphertext. NIST's methods are FF1 and FF3, and its 2025 draft revision drops FF3 after researchers "identified vulnerabilities when the number of possible inputs (i.e., the domain size) is sufficiently small".NIST, SP 800-38G Rev. 1, second public draft (February 2025): "The encryption method FF3 is no longer specified." A model doesn't check formats, so the proxy uses placeholders. Reach for FPE only when a system you can't change does.

3 · Finding it: three detectors

You can't mask what you don't find. Presidio, the open-source PII toolkit, combines "Named Entity Recognition, regular expressions, rule based logic and checksum", and still warns: "there is no guarantee that Presidio will find all sensitive information. Consequently, additional systems and protections should be employed."Presidio, "Main features" and "Warning". Presidio started at Microsoft and is now a community project; its docs moved to this address. Use detectors that fail in different places:

DetectorGood atMisses or gets wrong
Your own records: the CRM's contacts for this ticket's accountPeople Acme knows, in any spelling: "pieter" is Pieter BakkerAnyone not in the CRM, like Markus's colleague Lena
Patterns with a checksumEmails, IBANs, card numbers, phone numbersAnything without a fixed shape
NER, a model that tags names in textNames nobody told you aboutLower-case names, and words that look like names

Each mistake costs something different. A false negative leaks: the NER model in the exercise misses "pieter", so only the CRM catches him. A false positive breaks the agent: the same model tags "Florence" in "Florence 3" as a person with a score of 0.81, and an agent that reads "two [PERSON_1] 3 frames" can't start the return. A score threshold doesn't fix both: Lena, a real person, scores 0.58, below the mistake. The fix is an allow-list: Acme's product names and its own email domain are business data, so hits inside them don't count.

Let the format check itself. Card numbers and IBANs carry check digits. Presidio's card recognizer runs a Luhn check on every candidate, and its IBAN recognizer validates "using regex and checksum".Presidio source: credit_card_recognizer.py and iban_recognizer.py. Its supported entities table lists CREDIT_CARD as "Pattern match and checksum". Markus's email has two sixteen-digit numbers. 4111 1111 1111 1111 passes the Luhn check: it's a card. 2026 0000 1234 5678 fails it: it's his invoice number, and the agent needs it. Classify each run of digits once, as a whole, or a phone pattern will happily eat the last twelve digits of the invoice.

Detection is a first pass, not a promise. Some personal data has no shape at all: "Sorry for the slow reply, I was in hospital" matches no pattern and no name. Section 7 comes back to that.

4 · The proxy: placeholders in, values out

The design is Presidio's own pseudonymisation sample: replace each value with a placeholder like <PERSON_1>, keep the mapping, and "Since the user/client is holding the entity_mapping, it is possible to use it for de-anonymization as well."Presidio, "Use Presidio Anonymizer for Pseudonymization of PII data" (sample notebook). Four rules make it safe:

  1. Same value, same placeholder. Jan's IBAN appears twice, once with spaces. If it becomes [IBAN_1] and [IBAN_2], the model thinks he has two accounts. Normalise first (no spaces, lower-case emails, a first name maps to the contact's full name), then look the value up in the vault before you number a new one.
  2. The vault is per conversation. [IBAN_1] in Jan's conversation is his account. In Sanne's conversation it means nothing, and unmasking it there must fail. Otherwise a model that mixes up two threads, or an email that quotes a placeholder, can put Jan's account number in Sanne's reply.
  3. Unmask only at the two exits. Kim's review queue, because at Acme a person reads and sends every draft (the adoption number in Lesson 9 counts how often they use it), and the tool runner, where start_return needs the real IBAN. Nothing else gets real values. A placeholder the vault doesn't know for this conversation, like a made-up [PERSON_99], refuses the whole call.
  4. Log every unmask, but never the values. Who asked, why, which placeholders, and whether it was refused. A log that copied the values would be a second vault, and a less protected one.

The vault is now the most sensitive table you own. GDPR names "the pseudonymisation and encryption of personal data" as security measures.GDPR, Article 32(1)(a). The EDPB adds that pseudonymisation's effectiveness "is highly dependent on the choice of the pseudonymisation domain and its isolation from additional information".EDPB, Guidelines 01/2025, "Executive summary". So keep the vault where Acme's data may live (Lesson 2's data residency), encrypted, readable only by the proxy, and out of every log and trace. And delete rows when the conversation closes: GDPR says personal data is "kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary".GDPR, Article 5(1)(e), "storage limitation".

5 · Build it

The exercise lives in exercises/0011-pii/. The top of harness.ts describes the three emails, the tickets and contacts tables, the NER stand-in, the product catalogue and the unmask_log table. You write four functions: setup, mask, unmask and exportCase. The harness sends each masked email to a stand-in model that keeps a copy of everything it receives, like a provider's logs, and the checks read that copy.

start.ts is the proxy most people write first: two regular expressions, the NER model's names, and a map in memory.

Read what the provider saw. Half of Jan's IBAN, cut where the phone pattern happened to start. A bike model turned into a person. Two different placeholders for one bank account, so the model can't tell they're the same, and the draft never mentions the refund. And the golden-set copy looks clean, but every placeholder in it is a key the vault still holds.

Fix start.ts. The TODO list in the file follows the checks. Give yourself 35 minutes before you open the solution.

  1. Three detectors: the CRM contacts for this ticket's account (full name and first name, any case), patterns with checksums, and every findNames() hit.
  2. An allow-list: no name inside a product name, no Acme address.
  3. Classify each whole run of digits once: Luhn-valid card, phone, or keep. IBANs pass mod 97. When hits overlap, the first and longest wins.
  4. One placeholder per normalised value, numbered per conversation, kept in a vault table.
  5. unmask looks up this conversation only, and refuses the call if any placeholder is unknown.
  6. One unmask_log row per call, with the placeholders, never the values.
  7. exportCase: the same detection, a label with no number, nothing stored.

6 · The golden-set copy

Placeholders are right for a live conversation, because Kim and the tools need the values back. They are wrong for a copy that will be kept, shared with the eval team, and maybe sent to a vendor: anyone with the vault can put the names back, so the copy is still personal data. exportCase() runs the same detection and writes [PERSON] and [IBAN], with no number and nothing stored.

That is still not anonymisation by itself. GDPR asks about "all the means reasonably likely to be used, such as singling out".GDPR, Recital 26. "A bike shop in Utrecht with a workshop and an event on Saturday" might single out Sanne without her name. HIPAA's guidance says the same about free text: clinical notes "may provide context that readily allows for patient identification".HHS de-identification guidance, section 3.10, free text fields. So a person from Luis's team reads every case before it goes in, as they did when they wrote the expected answers in Lesson 8. The detectors do the bulk; the person does the judgement.

7 · A bank or a hospital

This stage is marked specialist because its heavy version comes with regulated customers. The proxy stays the same. What changes is the data, the contracts, and who decides.

Acme (bikes)A bankA hospital
Most sensitive dataIBANs, the odd card numberAccount and card numbers, everywhereHealth data: diagnoses, notes, scans
Rules on top of GDPRThe contract with AcmeCard industry and banking rules; ask their security team which applyGDPR's special category data; in the US, HIPAA
Before the model sees anythingMask, ask for ZDRMask; FPE where an old system checks formatsA signed BAA with the provider, then mask
What detection misses"I was in hospital"Free-text notes about a customer's financesAlmost everything that matters: notes, dates, rare conditions

Health data is a different category. Under GDPR, processing "data concerning health" is prohibited unless one of a list of exceptions applies.GDPR, Article 9(1) and 9(2). In the US, HIPAA lists 18 identifiers that must go for data to count as de-identified under its "Safe Harbor" method, including names, every date "directly related to an individual" except the year, phone numbers, email addresses and "Account numbers", and the list applies "regardless of its location in a record", free text included.HHS de-identification guidance, "The De-identification Standard" and section 3.10. Masking five types, as Acme does, is nowhere near that.

Masking doesn't remove the contract. HHS on cloud providers: one that handles a hospital's health data "is a business associate", and "This is true even if the CSP processes or stores only encrypted ePHI and lacks an encryption key for the data."HHS, "Guidance on HIPAA & Cloud Computing". CSP: cloud service provider. So the hospital signs a BAA with the model provider first. Some providers offer one: Anthropic's API supports PHI "With a signed BAA and a HIPAA-enabled organization".Anthropic, "API and data retention", "HIPAA readiness", checked November 2026. The proxy then adds data minimisation on top. It never replaces the contract.

You don't decide this alone. In every row, the customer's data protection officer, security team and lawyers decide what may leave, and where to. Your job is to make their decision true in code, show them what the model sees, and tell them plainly what detection misses. A list of known gaps earns more trust than a claim of "100% PII removal".

10 · Answer Petra

Petra asked what personal data reaches the model provider, and why. Write the answer she can put in Acme's records. Write it for her, not for an engineer. It saves as you type.
